Privacy Policy — Alchohalt

Effective date: [OWNER: set at submission]  Last updated: 2026-09-17

TL;DR

What you log stays on your phone by default. Alchohalt has no account system and we operate no server that could receive your data. Everything you enter — drink log, mood tags, HALT triggers, journal, goals, settings — lives only in your device's local storage.

The only third parties that ever see anything related to your use of Alchohalt are:

  1. Apple App Store / Google Play — when you buy Premium, they process your payment. They tell us only that an anonymous purchase is valid. They never tell us your name, card, or anything you logged.
  2. RevenueCat (purchase-validation broker) — at the moment you purchase or restore, they receive an anonymous app-instance ID and a product ID so we can confirm your premium entitlement is real. They never see what you log.
  3. Our AI model provider — only if you opt in to AI Insights (your first insight is free; unlimited insights are a premium feature). Off by default. Turning it on requires explicit consent, and only an anonymized pattern summary ever leaves the device. Your raw data — journal text, notes, exact times, custom drink names — never leaves the device, even with AI Insights on. See Section 5.

There is no account, no sign-up, and no email. Nobody — including us — can see your entries, because they never leave your phone.

1. What we collect

On your device only (not transmitted anywhere)

  • Drink log entries (timestamp, drink type, amount, cost, intention, craving level, HALT flags)
  • Goals (daily/weekly limits, monthly budget)
  • Journal entries (text + emoji + mood tag)
  • Streak counts and statistics
  • App preferences (language, theme, reminder settings)

This data lives in your device's local storage — Capacitor Preferences on iOS / Android, localStorage on web. It is not encrypted at rest by the app — it relies on your device's own protections (device screen lock and OS sandboxing). We have no remote copy. If you uninstall the app without making a backup, we cannot recover your data because we never had it.

Files you create (you hold them; we never see them)

  • Encrypted backup (free): a .alch-backup file sealed on your device with a passphrase you choose (Argon2id key derivation + XChaCha20-Poly1305). Your passphrase never leaves the device. If you forget it, the backup cannot be opened — by you, by us, by anyone. That is by design.
  • Plaintext export (free): JSON and CSV files you save wherever you choose. These are not encrypted — treat them like any sensitive file. Once exported, protecting them is your responsibility.

Both produce a file you hold. You may save or share the file through your device's share sheet to your own cloud storage (e.g. iCloud Drive, Google Drive) or email it to yourself — that choice is yours, and we are never a party to it. There is no live sync: the app has no server, so nothing updates anywhere automatically.

What we do NOT collect

  • Your name, email, phone number, or address — there is no account system at all: no login, no sign-up, no profile.
  • Your IP address (beyond what Apple/Google's payment infrastructure inherently sees during a purchase).
  • Cookies, tracking pixels, advertising IDs.
  • Analytics (PostHog, Mixpanel, Google Analytics, Firebase Analytics — none of these are integrated, and a source-level test enforces it).
  • Your precise location or GPS data — ever, in any feature.

2. How we use what we collect

The purchase-token data flowing through Apple/Google/RevenueCat is used only to:

  • Verify that your premium subscription is active when you open the app.
  • Restore your premium access if you delete and reinstall the app.

We do not sell, share, rent, or otherwise transfer your data to anyone else. We have no advertising partners.

3. Data retention

  • On your device: retained until you delete the app or use the "Wipe all data" control in Settings (double-confirm). Either action removes every copy we could ever reach — there is no server-side copy.
  • Apple / Google: retained per their respective privacy policies.
  • RevenueCat: retained per their privacy policy (https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy). Typically the purchase record is kept for the lifetime of your subscription plus the accounting period they require.

We retain nothing on our own servers because we don't operate any.

4. Your rights (GDPR / UK GDPR / CCPA-CPRA / VCDPA / CPA / CTDPA)

Under applicable data-protection laws you have rights to access, port, correct, and erase your data, and to be free from discrimination for exercising them. Because, by default, all of your data is on your own device:

  • Access / portability: Settings → Backup → "Plaintext export & import" (JSON/CSV), or the free encrypted backup.
  • Correction: edit any entry directly in the app.
  • Erasure: Settings → "Wipe all data" (double-confirm). Because your data lives only on this device, this removes every copy we could ever reach. If you saved a backup or export file, delete that file too — it is the only other copy, and only you hold it.
  • Opt out of "sale" / "sharing" / targeted advertising: there is nothing to opt out of — we do not sell or share your data and we run no advertising. (California CCPA/CPRA, Virginia VCDPA, Colorado CPA, Connecticut CTDPA.)

For the purchase-token slice held by Apple/Google/RevenueCat, those parties are the relevant data controllers/processors for those records; you can also contact them directly.

To exercise any right against us, email the address in Section 8 — note that for on-device data we have nothing to retrieve on our side, so the fastest path is the in-app export / wipe controls above. We do not charge a fee for, or discriminate against, exercising these rights.

Residents of Washington, Nevada, Colorado, and Connecticut have additional consumer-health-data rights — see the Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy.

5. Optional features that change this posture

Crash reporting — OFF by default

The app contains an opt-in crash reporter (Settings → Privacy & data). It is off by default. The reporter is a no-op unless a crash-reporting endpoint is configured in the build — in the current release none is configured, so nothing is transmitted even if you opt in. If a future release configures an endpoint, enabling crash reporting (opt-in) would send only sanitized crash metadata — error message, stack trace (file + line), OS family, app version, and timestamp. Your wellness data — drink entries, mood, journal, notes, tags — is never included in a crash report. You can disable it from Settings at any time.

Encrypted backup — a file you create and hold

You can export an encrypted backup file (.alch-backup) from Settings → Backup. It is sealed on your device (Argon2id key derivation + XChaCha20-Poly1305) with a passphrase you choose. The app never uploads it and we never receive it. Your passphrase never leaves the device. Without it, nobody — including us — can read the file. If you forget the passphrase, the backup is unrecoverable; that is the point of the encryption.

There is no account, no server, and no sync service: this exported file is the only way your data can exist off the device, and you are the only one who holds it. Where you keep the file — your phone, your own iCloud Drive or Google Drive, an email to yourself — is entirely your choice, made through your device's share sheet.

AI Insights — an opt-in feature, OFF by default

AI Insights is off by default, and turning it on requires explicit, informed consent (a consent screen listing exactly what is sent and what is never sent, plus an "I understand" affirmation). Your first insight is free — after you have logged enough days for the patterns to be meaningful, you may generate one insight without a Premium subscription. Unlimited insights require an active Premium subscription. The design contract, which this policy enforces:

  • An anonymized summary leaves the device only when you tap "Generate insights" — never in the background. It travels through our server-side proxy to the AI model provider we have configured (Anthropic by default; we may switch providers, e.g. to OpenAI, without changing what is sent). This applies equally to the one free insight and to Premium generations.
  • The only fields that may ever leave the device are the allowlist in src/lib/ai/types.ts (SanitizedAIPayload): ISO-week drink buckets (no exact timestamps), mood-tag counts, HALT trigger counts, intention counts, occasion counts (who you were with: alone / friends / family / out), morning-reflection "was it worth it?" counts (yes / meh / no — counts only), "was it worth it?" broken down by occasion (for evenings with a reflection: satisfaction counts per occasion logged that evening — counts only, e.g. alone: {yes: 1, meh: 2, no: 3}), day-of-week counts, streak length, a rotating anonymous instance ID, and locale.
  • Never sent: your name, email, or address (we never collect these); any free text (journal entries, drink notes, reflection notes); custom drink names; exact timestamps; location; or any persistent device identifier.
  • Whether your one free insight has been used is recorded on our proxy as an anonymous flag tied to your rotating instance ID — no account, no identity.
  • Consent can be revoked at any time in Settings, which will wipe the anonymous instance ID locally and stop any in-flight request.

If you never opt in, no AI-related data leaves your device, period.

6. Children

Alchohalt is rated 17+ because it tracks alcohol consumption. It is not directed at, and not intended for, children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 17. Because the app has no account system and stores data on-device, we do not knowingly hold data from children under 13 within the meaning of the U.S. Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) — there is no server-side record for us to hold. If a child has used the app, the data is on that device only; uninstalling the app or using "Wipe all data" removes it.

The current version is always available inside the app at Settings → About & support → Legal, and at the public legal URL referenced by the App Store / Google Play listing. The "Last updated" date at the top of this document reflects the most recent change.
